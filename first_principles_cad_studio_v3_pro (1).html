MILLING RESOLUTION SPECIFICATION

1. Chordal Deviation Principle

When milling cylindrical features, any chordal approximation produces physical faceting. The maximum perpendicular deviation $\delta$ between a true circular arc of radius $R$ and its planar chord segment is given by $$\delta = R \left( 1 - \cos\left( \frac{\theta}{2} \right) \right)$$ By setting the chordal target to sub-micron thresholds (e.g., $5\mu\text{m}$), we dynamically compute the minimum polygon segment count $N$: $$N = \left\lceil \frac{\pi}{\arccos\left(1 - \frac{\delta}{R}\right)} \right\rceil$$

2. Winding & C2 Spline Continuity

Discrete pixel steps from image tracing translate into devastating vibrations and tool wear during CNC toolpath generation. This studio reconstructs traced boundaries using Catmull-Rom interpolation to preserve tangency and continuity, ensuring smoother tool motion and reduced cutter load. $$\mathbf{P}(t) = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & t & t^2 & t^3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & -5 & 4 & -1 \\ -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} P_0 \\ P_1 \\ P_2 \\ P_3 \end{bmatrix}$$

3. Mechanical Integrity Check

Our model evaluates watertight correctness by checking bounding volumes and preventing face overlapping. This outputs perfect, clean manifold STL mesh primitives suitable for fabrication and simulation workflows.
